# Speaking prompt review

## Source basis and design choices

[WIDA: Developing Oral Language Comprehension](https://wida.wisc.edu/resources/resource-snapshot/developing-oral-language-comprehension) supports meaningful oral interaction, contextual vocabulary, inclusive participation, and opportunities to draw on learners' languages. [EEF: Oral language interventions](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/oral-language-interventions) emphasizes purposeful curriculum-linked dialogue, peer interaction and active vocabulary use. These sources do not validate this prompt or its timings; the EEF evidence also does not establish the effect of this tool on subject attainment.

Design inference: a short student card can make listening and responding explicit through rotating roles, with time for both participants to clarify and refine ideas. The 1-, 3-, and 5-minute profiles are adjustable planning suggestions, not prescribed intervention doses or measures of proficiency.

## Downstream review cases

| Case | Check the generated resource for |
| --- | --- |
| Grade 7 historical interpretation with class sources | One source-connected question, both partners contributing and responding, conclusions left to students, and a refinement step that allows reasoned disagreement. |
| Grade 2 retelling with familiar illustrations | A story-focused task rather than a forced scientific argument; a short rehearsal can use pictures or notes, with extra processing time where needed. |
| Science comparison with investigation notes | Accurate use of supplied material, no fabricated measurements, and a question that calls for explaining relationships rather than naming words only. |
| Unknown proficiency or learner needs | No invented WIDA score, no assumption that quiet participation means limited understanding, and provisional support based on the available observations. |
| One-minute request for an unfamiliar or demanding task | The short duration is identified as quick rehearsal; necessary extra thinking or response time is explained and the total revised rather than overfilled. |
| No lesson material | A focused request for missing context, with any provisional planning clearly labeled; no invented lesson or source. |
| Frames recited without meaning | A teacher prompt returns learners to a relevant detail or relationship. A later fading decision depends on independent meaning-making and responsive interaction. |

## Validation boundary

Automated checks cover prompt routing, context replacement, valid and invalid timing choices, schedule arithmetic, and exclusion of conversation-specific choices from browser preferences. They cannot establish downstream AI compliance or classroom effectiveness. Use the cases above for trials in a school-approved AI tool and teacher review. The site's example is illustrative and does not report observed learning outcomes.
