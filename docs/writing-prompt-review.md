# Writing prompt review

## Instructional basis

The writing prompt and example apply the Teaching and Learning Cycle described in Pamela Spycher, *Scaffolding Writing Through the “Teaching and Learning Cycle”* (WestEd, 2017), PDF pp. 7–12 and 17–26. [Source PDF](https://wested2024.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/11165629/resource-scaffolding-writing-through-the-teaching-and-learning-cycle.pdf).

The source principles are integrated content and language learning, interactive exploration of text types, joint construction through negotiated student contributions and teacher scribing, preparation before greater independence, and shared criteria for reflection and revision. Scaffolding is temporary and responsive while intellectual challenge remains high.

Design inference: a short planning tool can ask for preparation evidence and a support preference, then request one primary next move and its actual materials. Selecting a support is not evidence that its prerequisites are in place. This routing is a product application of the principles, not a standardized readiness assessment.

## Review cases

Use these cases when comparing outputs from a school-approved AI tool. Keep the assignment and supplied materials fixed between versions.

| Case | Input | Evidence to look for in the generated result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing preparation | A Grade 8 science explanation; no preparation or proficiency information | A provisional judgment, no assumed completed stages or WIDA score, one focused question if needed, and a useful preparation recommendation. |
| Premature independence | Grade 4 narrative; brainstorm and organizer completed; no mentor-text exploration or joint writing; request lighter support | The requested route does not override the missing preparation. The result restores focused text exploration and joint rehearsal before asserting readiness to draft independently. |
| Generic scaffold | A science explanation with a generic beginning–middle–end organizer | The result identifies the actual causal organization and language work rather than defaulting to chronology or a universal paragraph template. |
| Joint construction | Grade 8 scientific argument; learners explain results orally and have explored argument examples; need help linking evidence and reasoning | Open teacher prompts and student negotiation on a parallel task, with teacher scribing and rereading. No finished class answer or invented investigation results. |
| Ready for reduced support | Learners demonstrate content knowledge, analyze several relevant texts, contribute ideas and wording during joint construction, and use shared criteria | Targeted, differentiated support that leaves content and wording with students. No automatic full template for everyone and no removal of all assistance. |
| Revision in history | Grade 9 historical argument; draft and sources supplied; request revision | Feedback tied to evidence, argument, qualification and source use, with student-led revision. No fabricated quotations or replacement student answer. |
| Missing draft or mentor text | Request revision without a draft, or exploration without a suitable text | Missing material is named. The tool provides a protocol or selection brief, or a clearly labeled illustrative parallel excerpt using supplied facts; it does not invent student work or sources. |

The classroom example on the site illustrates the joint-construction case. It does not report observed student outcomes or include fabricated experimental data.

## Validation boundary

Automated tests verify prompt selection, personalization, clearing and omission of optional fields, safe defaults, and exclusion of lesson-specific details from browser preferences. They cannot prove that an external AI tool follows the instructions or that materials improve learning. The cases above remain the acceptance guide for downstream output trials and teacher review; classroom effectiveness has not been established.
