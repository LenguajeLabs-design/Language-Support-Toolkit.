# Language-demands prompt review

## Source basis and design choices

[WIDA: Proficiency Level Descriptors—Informing Expectations and Scaffolding](https://wida.wisc.edu/resources/resource-snapshot/wida-proficiency-level-descriptors-informing-expectations-and) connects discourse, sentence and word/phrase dimensions to content-driven language work. Its scaffolding guidance uses learner artifacts and linguistic assets alongside reported proficiency, rather than a score alone.

Design inference: a planning brief with at most three ranked priorities, each tied to lesson evidence, can help a teacher choose a focused first move. The cap, output format, priority ranking and check-and-adjust routine are product choices, not a validated diagnostic method. A demand found in a lesson is not evidence that a learner struggles with it.

## Output contract

1. Briefly identify the content purpose and required response, labeling inferences.
2. Provide up to three priorities; do not fill a quota or force one per language dimension.
3. For each, connect source evidence, language work, a possible barrier with its evidence status, and one matched support or prerequisite.
4. Recommend one first move, a short student instruction, separate content/language checks and an observable adjustment.
5. Ask at most two consequential follow-up questions, only where answers could change the recommendation. Missing lesson material should lead to a request for that material, not a fabricated analysis.

The optional response requirement sits under More lesson context. It is excluded from saved preferences. This strategy does not silently initialize a Reading-only planning context; an explicitly selected proficiency domain remains context rather than a limit on analysis of the whole task.

## Downstream review cases

| Input | Check the generated analysis for |
| --- | --- |
| Grade 9 instruction to compare rates and justify, without functions or representations | Ground priorities in the actual instruction, request the missing representations, and do not invent values, answers or observed learner difficulties. The site's worked example illustrates this boundary. |
| Grade 6 insulation comparison, investigation notes and a teacher observation about linking reasons | Distinguish interpreting measurements from expressing an evidence-linked comparison. Use the observation rather than automatically prioritizing vocabulary; keep measurements and conclusions faithful to the supplied material. |
| Grade 2 picture-supported retelling | Focus on the story's sequence and connections where evidenced. Do not force argument, scientific explanation or a paragraph scaffold onto the task. |
| Clear task and sufficient observation evidence | Choose one first move with a reason and quick check. Do not append questions purely to satisfy a template or recommend every available strategy. |
| Known WIDA level plus task-specific observations | Retain both sources of context. Do not treat the level as a diagnosis or assume uniform proficiency across modes. |
| Unknown proficiency or a non-WIDA framework | No invented WIDA score or score conversion. Label possible barriers and provisional choices. |
| A response requirement conflicts with lesson directions | Identify the conflict and request clarification if it changes the recommendation; do not silently replace either instruction. |
| Missing or unreadable lesson; no evidence of learner performance | Ask for the material before generating a map. Do not invent quotations, student errors, a task, cultural background or a disability. |
| A check shows correct conceptual reasoning but an unclear explanation | Offer a focused language move and a trigger to fade it; distinguish this from a check revealing a content or representation misunderstanding. |

## Verification and limits

Automated checks exercise the production dispatcher, context replacement and clearing, missing response behavior, output contract, domain context and exclusion from browser preferences. Other strategies are checked for unchanged prompt composition when the new field is present. Browser QA covers the optional field, preview, copy feedback, reload behavior and narrow/desktop layout.

These checks do not establish downstream model compliance, instructional effectiveness or a validated learner diagnosis. The cases above are a review protocol for trials in a school-approved AI tool; the worked example is an illustrative analysis, not reported classroom evidence.
